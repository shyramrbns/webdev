        General camera guide

        Mode dial

        Auto lets the camera choose everything. P (program) sets exposure while you adjust the rest. A (aperture priority) lets you set the aperture and the camera picks the shutter speed. S (shutter priority) is the reverse. M (manual) gives you all three. Movie is for video.

        Aperture, shutter speed and ISO

        Aperture (the f-number) controls how much light enters: a lower number lets in more light and blurs the background more. Shutter speed controls how long the sensor is exposed: fast speeds freeze motion, slow ones blur it. ISO brightens the image electronically: higher values help in the dark but add noise.

        Autofocus basics

        Press the shutter button halfway to focus. Single AF locks focus once, which suits still subjects. Continuous AF keeps refocusing, which suits moving subjects. Eye AF, where a camera offers it, follows a person’s or animal’s eye.

        Changing lenses

        Switch the camera off, hold the lens release button and turn the lens to remove it. Fit the new lens by lining up the marks and turning until it clicks. Keep the body facing down while the mount is open so dust stays out.

        Handling and care

        Hold the grip with your right hand and support the lens with your left. Keep caps on when you are not shooting, clean the lens with a blower and a microfibre cloth, and never touch the sensor.
